# Book 2: Carl's Doomsday Scenario (Dungeon Crawler Carl, Matt Dinniman)

Reference summary for the 2D pixel-art side-scroller team. Line numbers like [L1200] point into the source markdown `DCC_02.md` (7,035 lines). Everything is paraphrased; where the book was unclear it is flagged "(unsure)".

---

## 1. Overview

**Overview.** Book 2 covers Floor 3 of the World Dungeon, the first "Over City" floor. Floors 1 and 2 were corridors and caves; Floor 3 is a theme-park-style, above-ground-looking medieval city under a fake sky. The floor lasts only eight days (shortened from the usual length), and the book spans about six in-game days. Carl and Donut now pick a race and class (a major change from book 1), gain a "manager" (Mordecai teleports into every saferoom with them), and take on their first quests, elites, neighborhood/borough/city bosses, and a pet that grows from a puppy-sized raptor to a pony-sized one. Tone shifts: more serialized "TV drama" plots written by producers, and more consequences from fame (viewer counts in the quadrillions, assassination attempts from orbit).

**What carries over from book 1:** Carl (human, ex-Coast Guard, boat-yard worker, barefoot because the system will not let him wear shoes; the AI is fond of his feet), Princess Donut (Persian cat, now sapient, huge Charisma, rides on Carl's shoulders/neck), Mongo (the baby velociraptor adopted at the end of book 1), Mordecai (the guide), Zev (Odette's show assistant who now acts as their agent), Odette (talk-show host, Valtay/Kua-tin media world), the Desperado Club tattoo/pass, the Nightgaunt Cloak, magical gauntlet, BigBoi Boxers, Rev-Up Moonshine and Boom Jugs, the slingshot, saferooms, loot boxes, achievements, recap episodes and the countdown timer. Brandon's group (care-home residents) and Maestro / Skull Empire / Borant / Valtay politics continue in the background.

**What changes:** Race and class selection, "manager" benefit, quests tab, elites (scripted drama NPCs), neighborhood/borough/city boss tiers, NPCs that are engineered living beings, day/night cycle with a janitor mob, shops and paid saferoom rooms, party sharing experience, the Hobby Potion, fan boxes, the leaderboard with bounties, and a new weapon/explosives playstyle for Carl (xistera, hob-lobbers).

**Plot summary (spoiler-complete).** On arrival at Floor 3, Mordecai (now an Incubus) walks Donut and Carl through race/class selection. Donut tricks Carl and Mordecai into picking the "Former Child Actor" class that gives her the Manager benefit; Carl becomes a Primal Compensated Anarchist (explosives, traps, punching). They enter the Over City, find a tiny village, then walk into the ruins and trigger a quest to end a mutated ruined circus (Grimaldi's Traveling Circus). They meet the elite Tsarina Signet, a half-naiad tattoo summoner who tries to use Carl's blood as her sacrifice. She later kidnaps them, puts Donut to sleep with a curse, and forces Carl to kill the circus boss Heather the Bear to power her paper-thin "Blood and Ink" army. Inside the big top, Carl meets city boss Ringmaster Grimaldi (Pestiferous Vine). He makes a deal with the show's producers through Zev, fakes the poison kill, and talks Grimaldi into releasing his "banished" daughter Signet; the circus is gone by morning and reveals a stairwell. At the medium skyfowl town, Carl and Donut shop, visit the Desperado Club and the Silk Road market, are rewarded with fan boxes (a jai-alai xistera for Carl; a photo of Carl's ex for Donut), and take a second quest after the death of an orc named GumGum: find out why dead prostitutes are falling from the sky. They are televised on "Danger Zone" where Carl insults the Skull Empire king; a missile strike meant for them kills singer Manasa, and the Valtay blow up the royal yacht. Back in town they fight the city elf militia (the 201st), blow up the magistrate's building, kill the real culprit Miss Quill (a lich-like skyfowl schemer) and uncover her undead husband Remex, powering a soul crystal. Their quest triggers a town-wide magical explosion; Carl contains it in a glass case, strips naked, and leads a group of crawlers to a stairwell. They escape to Floor 4 early. Odette's show reveals that the producers' appeal and veto cost them all prizes, that a rival crawler got a free Celestial box, and that they are 6th and 7th on a new leaderboard, with bounties on their heads.

## 4. World and System rules (new or changed in this book)

- **Race and class** are picked in the guildhall at the start of Floor 3. Options are presented with 3 AI recommendations. Earth races/classes give a Silver Earth Box. Some classes need achievements or view counts (e.g., Former Child Actor needs "Cut!" and 1 trillion views; Compensated Anarchist needs Explosives 5 and 500B views). Stat minimums can force spending points. The Desperado pass excludes cleric/paladin classes. Roughly 80% of crawlers stay human.
- **Primal race:** blank slate, minus 1 to all stats, all skills can reach level 20 (normal cap 15 except by special sources). Level-16+ skills are multiplicatively stronger.
- **Character Actor skill:** choose a specialty class each floor; the AI rolls how many skills you get; resets when the floor ends.
- **Manager benefit:** guide becomes a teleporting manager who can chat and help but cannot fight; cannot access codex; protected by system from Floor 4 (death sends him out, not killed); he earns a bonus the longer the crawler lives.
- **Floor 3 is the Over City:** 8-day timer, size about Arizona, day/night cycle. Village guards (Level 75 Swordsmen) only work in daytime and are collectively minded. Random placement of crawlers. Villages are tiny/small/medium/large/XL; medium ones have a tutorial guild. Saferooms are pubs (rooms cost gold now: 10-20g per night). Hologram signposts show stairs, restrooms, saferooms.
- **Janitor mob** at night: Street Urchins.
- **Quests tab:** quests come from NPCs or area discovery. Reward box tier is shown (Silver/Gold/Platinum). Winning a quest can be a "cheap" or "elaborate" path; the producers can hot-patch elites.
- **Elites:** scripted drama NPCs with plot armor; shown as dots with a black cross. They are "designed" not natural; killing them may fail quests. Charm effects: breaking your own finger counters Puppy Dog charm.
- **Boss tiers:** Neighborhood Boss (one star), Borough Boss (two stars), City Boss (very deadly, nobody survives yet). Neighborhood bosses give map upgrades and a bronze boss box.
- **NPCs** are engineered living beings owned by Borant; memories get rewritten each season. Killing NPCs gives zero experience now.
- **Hobby Potion:** gives a level-3 skill in an Earth hobby; random, may be useless.
- **Fan boxes:** unlocked at one quadrillion views; fans vote for the contents (trolls can win). Delay of 30 hours.
- **Fleeting items** disappear when removed (tiara, Fang Caps).
- **Short-shelf-life items** (Scroll of Upgrade) expire in 20 minutes.
- **Safe-room mail:** Floor-of-the-book spellbooks arrive in a mailbox.
- **Muted zones** disable menu spells and scrolls but not items or potions.
- **Bounties and leaderboard:** top-10 list; each name has a bounty payable to anyone who kills them. Odette says the formula blends views, favorites, levels, and money.
- **Party** shares experience; far-away allies get less. Chat handshake (fist bump) in person is needed.
- **Event quest:** can force all crawlers in a blast zone to participate; saferooms locked; reward Platinum Quest Box.
- **Achievement upgrade chain:** Hadji upgrades a quest box one tier; Bandit upgrades two tiers (Celestial). Both patched out afterward.
- **Sponsorship** officially starts Floor 4; collective bidding exists.
- **Desperado Club:** separate plane shared by all club locations across the floor; mostly bots; has casino, quest hall, market, guild-o-rama; Club Vanquisher is the rival club for clerics.
- **Tourists** are on Floor 18; hunters enter at Floor 6 (Hunting Grounds). Gods appear starting Floor 4.
- **Potions** can be combined (cinnamon + thistle rot heals pets; Satch Toad extract + healing cures naiad sleep; Hair of the Dog counters alcohol).
- **Manager protection** starts Floor 4. Staff in the first three floors have no protection.

## 5. Floors, levels and locations (most important for artists)

### 5.1 Arrival hallway and Mordecai's guildhall (the "green room" exit)
- **Look:** long golden-brick hallway with a plush red carpet, ceiling painted as a dark star-filled nebula (it is a fake sky; ceiling about 25 feet up and "plink" if hit). Ends at the familiar door to Mordecai's guild room.
- **Inside:** Mordecai's classroom/lounge now tidy, with two beds for race transformation, fireplace, shelf, desk, chairs; the screen floats mid-air for menus.
- **Tone:** quiet comedy. Mordecai is in a tuxedo, then rips it off. The door opens onto the village. Already built in the game as Level 0.

### 5.2 The Over City villages (small town at start)
- **Look:** medieval fantasy town built on wooden slat streets with gaps; sulfur smoke drifts up from holes (volcano below). A fake blood-red sun hangs on the ceiling, bathing everything in crimson light. Buildings: half-timber shops, pubs, inns and guildhalls; four-story buildings visible in the smoky distance; ceiling is a rocky illusion that shows lighting artifacts.
- **People:** dwarves, humans, orcs, elves; faceless full-plate guards (Village Guard Swordsman, Lv75).
- **Rules:** guards do not leave the village and only work in daytime; killing NPCs is death penalty. Small waist-high fence and gate separate village from ruins.
- **Tone:** busy, grimy theme park; polluted warm air; shops overpriced.

### 5.3 The Ruins (open streets)
- **Look:** crumbling moss-covered brick buildings with no doors or windows, slumped roofs with rotting beams; wood-slat streets that can collapse into basements; fog drifting at street level; alleys and dead ends; abandoned parks; ceilings dark. The same "wooden ground" is a hazard (fall into cellars).
- **Day vs night:** day is red-sun light and manageable; at night everything is dark with distant fire glows and eerie screams; monsters spawn more; guards vanish.
- **Features:** hologram signposts growing from discolored rocks; restrooms; saferoom pubs (e.g., Belly-Rubbed Pug at the end of a dead-end alley); stairwells hidden until a related quest is completed.
- **Enemies:** circus mobs, lemurs, krasue, Shambling Berserkers, Street Urchins, Shadow Leaks, Ruin Flockers, brain boilers, an eyeball spider (beach-ball body), Mold Lions.
- **Tone:** creepy-funny.

### 5.4 The Belly-Rubbed Pug (saferoom pub)
- Renaissance-fair tavern with a pug-in-top-hat sign. Bopca (gnome) protector Gordo behind the bar. Five rentable rooms at 10 gold per night. Three TV screens show recap episodes. Mailbox in a corner for spellbooks. Decorated with a counter, tables, kitchen in back. Good set for comedy beats (Mordecai passed out in vomit).

### 5.5 Grimaldi's Traveling Circus (quest area)
- **Look:** a park in the middle of the ruins now a fortified camp: three huge tents (the center "Big Top" very large), many wagons, cages, a dozen smaller tents, walls of rocks, wood and bones 5 ft high draped in animal skins, jack-shaped wooden obstacles linked with barbed wire. Cauldron crews in tattered purple-and-yellow executioner robes fire glowing purple-yellow magical mortars. A steam calliope (coach-sized brass pipe organ) plays slow, haunting polka music. At night: spotlights sweep, fires burn, tents glow like a holiday display.
- **Freak show tent:** painted ten-foot banners ("World O' Freaks": Garth the Two-Headed Troglodyte, Tiny the Fire-Breathing Gnoll, the Strongest Citizen ogre). Poster of Tsarina Signet crossed out as "Traitor," clowns urinate on it.
- **Big Top interior:** entrance shaped like a giant clown face (walk through the mouth), threadbare blood-stained red carpet, faded white eyes. Inside: performers in perpetual chaos, juggling lemurs, unicycling clown eating a goblin leg, aerialists collide; a wooden Grimaldi cutout sign with greeting; empty bleachers; ice-cream stand run by Apollon. Center ring holds the giant pale-green Vine, roots like pythons sprawling above ground. Three support poles; one looks like popsicle sticks.
- **Gimmicks:** Big Top ticket grants safe passage unless you enter the rings or turn violent.
- **After:** by morning tents disappear and a stairwell to Floor 4 appears in the park.
- **Tone:** carnival horror with black humor; the smell of nostalgia hits Carl.

### 5.6 Signet's white building
- Tall white ruined building; first floor an empty shell with broken walls, rotting cushioned chair where Donut sleeps, cage for Mongo. Used as a base; the neighborhood is quiet, near the circus.

### 5.7 The Medium Skyfowl Settlement
- **Look:** four times bigger than the first village, taller buildings with large open windows for flying eagle-folk, many shops on upper floors; skyfowl swoop under the ceiling; guards are same plate-armor Swordsmen. Distinct districts: market square, tutorial guild door in a plain building, Desperado Club tower, poorer residential streets, slums at edges, alleys. Pubs: The One-Eyed Narwhal (fat bald man unzipping pants on sign), Hot Schnitzel (flowers outside), Boll Weevil and Chowder Pub. Town hall tower with a high-end mall: airport-style shops (silk robes, hats, scrolls) staffed by abused non-bird clerks; red carpet; stairwell up to the magistrate's reception. The administrative building is camouflaged as a pile of shingled medieval buildings (Disneyland look).
- **Gimmicks:** no ground access to the municipal level (rope, Puddle Jumper). Anti-charm spells in quest locations.
- **Night:** shops close, guards vanish, alleys become deadly; krasue haunt alleys.
- **Warehouse (Swordsmen depot):** boxy two-story building across from the town hall. Ground floor holds hundreds of inactive plate-armor Swordsmen in formation, each trailing yellow aura to a point in the ceiling (like a terracotta army). Second floor contains the amber soul crystal and Remex.
- **Corrupt Temple / Apito Education Center:** hand-painted tree sign, shares a wall with a dwarf family's house; street full of kids.
- **Final fate:** municipal building collapses; swords stop; most skyfowl fall ill from the failing spell; blast epicenter marked on map.

### 5.8 The Desperado Club
- **Exterior:** 1920s New York art-deco; square concrete columns, semicircular relief with sunburst rays (Empire State style); takes an entire street, six stories tall; giant spinning neon knife logo; slogan "So fun it hurts." Rock-monster bouncers.
- **Public first room:** noisy local pub, packed. Quest board for tattoos.
- **Members-only room:** huge dance floor on a separate plane. Dubstep, seizure lights, smoke, floating neon speakeasy-font signs for rooms (Casino, The Hunting Grounds, Guild-O-Rama, Bitches, Penis Parade, Jobs, The Silk Road, Restrooms, and an unmarked back door). Retro-futuristic Blade Runner feel. About 200 mostly bot patrons; waitress in blue skin. Privacy bubble spell mutes the music.
- **Silk Road:** a farmer's market of single-row stalls; Spider Shields (200k gold), black bubbling potions, four-pincered robed merchants; hobgoblin explosives stand Hobgobs and Boom Sticks.
- **Pet playroom:** wrecked by Lucia Mar's dogs.
- **Rules:** not a saferoom; bouncers only in the main room and casino; Casino opens in a few days.

### 5.9 Production trailer / Danger Zone studio
- Rental trailer with bowl-shaped bed, porthole to open ocean (daylight, floating silver containers, probes, ships in sky). Jetsons-style cleaner treadmill. Studio: simple round table with glowing sign "Danger Zone with Ripper Wonton," audience bank, holograms of guests. Attack scene: studio vanishes, trailer rocks.

### 5.10 The Abandoned Pool (YMCA)
- Half-ruined former YMCA with a drained outdoor swimming pool containing the bloated corpse of The Divider (two-necked whale boss). Magical darkness fills the street; Torch is snuffed out by anti-magic bolts; Shambling Berserkers shamble out. Later covered in gore with a stench like a failed refrigerator.

### 5.11 Magistrate's quarters and krasue nest
- Reception area: oversized desk, bento box, hundreds of Beanie Baby plushies on shelves (some in glass cases), a perch instead of a chair. Magically locked metal door behind. Behind it: empty living chambers with T-shaped roosts and an empty mice cage, then a meat-locker room of dozens of naked women hanging by the ankles from the ceiling, heads dangling, purple energy swirling; in the center a huge bone dreamcatcher hoop holds the mummified body of the dead Magistrate. Muted zone. Floors sticky with gore.

### 5.12 Hidden stairwell building
- A small decrepit building three kilometers east of town (Katia crashes through its wall), staircase down to Floor 4; sign reads that this is where "the real fun begins." The door is oversized Kua-tin carving. "Mind the gap."

### 5.13 Odette's green room (epilogue)
- Floating on waves (they are on a boat); snack table, couch, bathroom. Loud audience applause; screens.

## 8. Humor, tone and recurring bits

- **Snarky System:** achievement text roasts the player and uses pop-culture or real-world cracks; item descriptions swear and mock. It occasionally quotes literature when you are near death and mocks trying to be fair.
- **Donut chat in all caps** and her deliberate "acting" lies; Mordecai's exasperation at "Goddamnit, Donut."
- **Mordecai's drinking** and fear of ruined pants; his "Daily BOLO" (be on the lookout) lists. Carl keeps repeating a private vow ("You will not break me") in tense moments.
- **Carl as trouble magnet:** he cannot resist quests; "Did you get the quest?" becomes a repeating gag.
- **Mongo gags:** eats everything, bites arms, throws up a salamander, hates baths, rushes in against orders.
- **Gross-out bits:** worm cones, clown gore, piles of body parts, bloating boss corpses.
- **Naked Carl:** freeballing boxers, toilet-paper loincloth on live TV.
- **Beanie Babies** hoarding; a stuffed lemur and quokka as collectibles.
- **Play calls:** Panic, Mold Lion, Mic Drop, Slime Time, Boomy Boy, Go Long. They are named like sports plays and the party must follow them without question.
- **Politics:** Carl's frog-in-boiling-water speech triggers real violence; later it is a "no politics" rule.
- **Short example quotes:** the "Yellow-Bellied Chickenshit" achievement's reward line is "Pussies don't get prizes" [L3089]; Donut's reply when asked for an opinion: "I'm a cat!" [L4753]; Carl's recurring vow: "You will not break me" [L2517].
- **Tone shift:** more serialized drama, quieter moments about Carl's mother and the circus smell, Bea's mother's advice, Donut's grief (she knows Bea is dead).

## 9. Ideas for the side-scroller game

1. **Over City street level:** Build parallax streets of wooden slat boardwalks with gaps; smoke puffs through the holes as a hazard (periodic steam jets that damage if stepped on). Crimson-red lighting from a ceiling sun; shop signs and gnome guards in the background.
2. **Day/night timer:** When the sun fades, switch palette from warm red/orange to dark blue-black with distant firelight; guards vanish and tougher mobs spawn. Use a small sun icon on the HUD.
3. **Village Guard Swordsman:** invulnerable, slow, large plate-armor sprite that blocks doors in daytime; the player must avoid or lure them away. Guards turn red when you attack an NPC.
4. **Circus level:** sequential set pieces: Lemur rooftop ambush (knife-throwers popping on windows), giraffe-riding lemurs, stilt clown fight, mortar crews that fire telegraphed purple-yellow shells that collapse platforms, calliope music that speeds when the party is spotted.
5. **Heather boss:** roller-skate boss that dashes horizontally (whisk sound telegraph), whips worms along the floor that the player must jump or smoke-bomb; phase two flammable puddle; ends with a custard-fireball ticket gamble.
6. **Mold Lion trap:** an arena mini-game where Carl's Protective Shell bounces lions into a wall and Donut revives them as zombie allies for short-lived minions.
7. **Big Top finale:** the player must hold a Big Top Ticket item (consumable shield) and walk past non-aggressive clowns; Vine boss uses the poison-blood puzzle (collect poison, bleed on roots, wait timer, then survive roots whipping).
8. **Janitor mob:** Street Urchins as rolling spike balls that only attack if you drop junk; throw scrap away to distract them for safe passage.
9. **Donut abilities:** Puddle Jumper teleports across short gaps (cooldown bar), Clockwork Triplicate spawns two Mongo decoys, Torch spell lights dark levels. Light mechanic: krasue flee from bright torch, darkness makes Shambling Berserkers stronger.
10. **Carl's xistera:** a charged throw mechanic with arc preview; throw oranges (comic) or hob-lobbers (explosive). Lit-fuse timer visible; do not throw blind.
11. **Desperado Club hub:** neon art-deco interior with floating signs, bubble-mute toggle that quiets music, casino roulette mini-game with the 100,000-gold chip, Quint dealing potions. Great for a safe hub between levels.
12. **Shop haggle mini-game:** Donut sells items; a Charisma meter vs shopkeeper level yields discounts; skyfowl merchants try to cheat.
13. **Krasue nest dungeon:** muted zone (spells disabled) with hanging bodies; the player uses torches and smoke curtains; Mongo bites heads. A visual of heads dangling and organs swinging.
14. **Boom puzzle:** hide dynamite in shelves; remote-detonate with Pus; give detonator to Katia as an escort puzzle.
15. **Precursor bursts final level:** timed run with four events: gear magic disabled (stat loss), weapons activate, armor dissolves, hotlist items auto-use. The HUD shows a countdown and the player strips gear to survive; ends in a mad dash to the stairwell with hidden bonus for rescuing NPCs.
16. **Achievements in the book's voice:** "Pants Are Overrated" (play 10 minutes without legwear), "Pet Not Dinner" (stop Mongo from eating an item), "Plot Armor? Never Heard Of It" (fail an elite quest the AI planned), "Lazarus-ish" (survive a big spell).
17. **Gags:** the pet carrier shaking; Mordecai teleporting into a saferoom mid-brush; Beanie Babies falling like dominoes; the AI complaining about unneeded detail.
18. **Enemy design cues:** use contrast of cartoon (clown pastel, circus red-yellow) with gore (bone-white skull faces). Paper-thin tattoo monsters as a 2D side-view creature that is literally flat; punch them through.
19. **Boss variety:** city boss as an unkillable scenery hazard (Vine); the player must solve the puzzle rather than fight.
20. **Level palette:** Over City red-orange and brown with sulfur yellow smoke; circus purple-yellow and red-white stripes; Desperado Club neon pink, teal and black on gold; krasue nest purple with pale flesh; final run bleached white explosion glow.
