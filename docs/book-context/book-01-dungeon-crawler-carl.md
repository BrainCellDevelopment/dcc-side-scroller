# Book 1: Dungeon Crawler Carl (Matt Dinniman, 2020)

Reference summary for the DCC side-scroller team. Line numbers like [L1200] refer to the source markdown file `DCC_01.md` (8,475 lines, with embedded images on their own lines). All text is paraphrased; the few short quotes are marked.

---

## 1. Overview

Dungeon Crawler Carl is a first-person LitRPG comedy-horror told by Carl, a 27-year-old ex-Coast Guard marine technician from Seattle. At 2:23 AM on a freezing January night, an alien megacorporation (the Borant Corporation, acting under the interstellar "Syndicate") crushes every interior space on Earth, turning the planet's buildings and people into raw material for a televised 18-level "World Dungeon". Carl survives only because he was outside, in boxers, a leather jacket and his ex-girlfriend's pink Crocs, chasing her prize Persian cat, Princess Donut. The book covers roughly the first two floors (about two weeks of in-world time) and ends as Carl and Donut prepare to descend to Floor 3.

The tone is brutal, gory and extremely sarcastic. The System AI narrates achievements, item descriptions and monster tooltips as a snarky game-show host who mocks Carl constantly (and has a running foot fetish). Underneath the jokes is a real story about people dying by the millions, an entertainment industry that profits from it, and Carl's stubborn decency ("you will not break me").

**Plot summary (spoilers).** Carl and Donut enter the dungeon on Floor 1, collect a pile of absurd achievements and loot boxes, and are nearly killed by a Goblin Murder Dozer before reaching a Tutorial Guild run by Mordecai, a rat-shaped NPC guide. A Legendary Pet Box and an Enhanced Pet Biscuit give Donut huge stats and the ability to speak. Carl, barefoot and pantsless the entire book, becomes a bare-knuckle brawler (later with an orc-steel gauntlet), while Donut fires Magic Missiles and wears a tiara that secretly dooms her to a political death-trap on Floor 9. They kill a series of themed "Neighborhood Bosses" (the Hoarder, the goblin war chieftain, the Juicer), dodge a pair of murderous crawlers (Frank Q and Maggie My), then find and defeat the Floor 1 Borough Boss, the Ball of Swine, with a team of elderly care-home survivors (Meadow Lark) using a homemade fortress. They are pulled onto an alien talk show hosted by Odette, then descend to Floor 2, where they fight a Krakaren (an anti-vax pyramid-scheme octopus boss) that escapes its lair, survive a Rage Elemental summoned by an old man's public urination (by tricking it into the stairwell, which disintegrates it), and escort the elders down to Floor 3 while losing a beloved nurse. After humiliating the sadistic orc prince "the Maestro" on his death-game show (and becoming the subject of a viral fan video about it), they adopt a vicious baby velociraptor named Mongo as Donut's pet and descend to Floor 3, with the setup of a hidden, dangerous class option and a suspicious old woman (Agatha) who carries alien tech.

## 4. World and System rules (game-relevant)

- **The setup.** The Syndicate (a huge interstellar government) seeds worlds with six starter species including humans, waits, then strips the planet. Borant Corporation (kua-tin) gets rights to run the dungeon as a TV show: Dungeon Crawler World: Earth. Crawlers are contestants and viewers watch across the galaxy.
- **18 floors.** Entrances halve each level (150,000 to 75,000 to ...), ending with 2 entrances and a single exit on Floor 18. Each floor collapses when its timer ends; anyone left dies. Timers: Floor 1 = 5 days, Floor 2 = 6 days (shortened from the expected 10). Mobs also "sleep" at collapse and reappear for the next dungeon.
- **Crawler stages.** Crawlers enter at Lv1 as Human with no class. On Floor 3 they choose race, then class, then spend stat points (3 per level; Donut gets auto-assigned points). Level ups do not give usable stats until then.
- **Stats.** STR, INT, CON, DEX, CHA (Wisdom is hidden). Stats are "mish-mash" abstractions: INT is mana and memory; CON affects potion cooldowns and healing speed. CHA lets Donut charm sapient mobs with non-red dots (non-boss, low level, early floors only).
- **HUD.** Health bar (top right), mana bar, countdown timer, minimap (bottom right; green = you, blue = crawlers, white = NPCs, red = mobs, X = corpses; fog of war until visited), a hotlist of 10 quick slots, and a notification folder. Tooltips appear when you focus on something. Health bars of enemies appear only after damage.
- **Inventory.** Dimensional and effectively unlimited. You can store anything you can lift for ~4 seconds, except living things. Time-locked (food never spoils). Potions stack to 999. Items keep fuse state when stored. Menus can sort by value (with the Determine Value skill) and keep a History tab.
- **Skills.** Thousands of skills (Breathing, Walking, Frogger, etc.) that rise with use. Skills may cross-boost (Pugilism, Unarmed Combat, Iron Punch, Powerful Strike).
- **Loot boxes.** Six tiers: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Legendary, Celestial. Types include Adventurer, Pet, Weapon, Apparel, Shoe, Goblin, Boss, Crowd Control, Looter, Brawler, Survivor's, Mechanic's, Sapper's, Asshole's, Beguiler, Lucky Bastard. They can only be opened in safe zones. Contents are not truly random: the system tailors prizes to entertain (pants never arrive). Patrons send Benefactor Boxes (rare, can include alien tech).
- **Achievements.** Triggered by almost anything; each gives a sarcastic description and sometimes a box.
- **Safe rooms and guildhalls.** Safe zones (all mob attacks negated, mobs teleport away if they attack, no crawler violence or theft). Three strikes for crawlers: ~100 s freeze, 1 hour freeze, then stripped and dropped in a mob nest. Each safe room has a gimmick (free cookies, Mana Toast, a mailbox, playground). Rooms close one hour before level collapse (added in Floor 2). Door will not open if mobs are outside. Tutorial Guilds are personal safe rooms, with the guide visible only to the crawler and press.
- **Bathrooms.** Doors scattered through hallways open to a tiny silver-toilet closet (litterbox for cats). These are the only places viewers cannot see. Public urination or defecation outside bathrooms eventually summons a Rage Elemental.
- **Viewers and sponsors.** Views, Followers, Favorites (limited per viewer), Patrons. From Floor 4 each crawler can have up to 3 patrons. Higher numbers mean better rewards. Interviews on side shows (Odette, Death Watch) are scheduled through admins.
- **Bosses.** Six tiers: Neighborhood, Borough, City, Province, Country, Floor. Each Floor-1 neighborhood has a themed boss lair; boss fights lock the door, play music, show a VS card, and announce loudly. Bosses are often abducted humans or alien troops turned into caricatures. They drop persistent items (Neighborhood Map reveals ~1 square mile; Borough Field Guide reveals mob levels in quadrants). Bosses may leave their rooms on Floor 2.
- **Janitor mobs.** Rats on Floor 1, Brindle Grubs on Floor 2 spawn from corpses (limit 5,000 per quadrant), eat remains, grow, pupate and hatch into Vespas. Burn or crush bodies.
- **Stairs.** Each floor has multiple stairwells; Borough/City bosses guard some. Descending early freezes you until collapse (lose followers); the optimal window is within 6 hours of collapse. Non-sapient mobs that descend are disintegrated (exploit used by Carl, later patched). Descending restores health fully.
- **Death.** No respawn. Dead crawlers drop everything but unopened boxes. Corpses become lootable by others after about 10 minutes.
- **Languages and tech.** The System translates all languages: Carl hears Donut's speech as English, she hears his speech normally, and mobs hear her as squeaks or hisses; later patches add cross-language support for all human languages. Illegal tech (Valtay comm relay) is flagged as unusable.
- **Admins.** Kua-tin staff who appear and turn off all menus; no weapons allowed near them (execution). They may appear in person.

## 5. Floors, levels, and locations

### Level 0 (already built in the game): surface and entry

Map the following to the existing tutorial.

- **The collapsed street (surface).** [L61-L217] Pitch dark, freezing, overcast night in Seattle, 2:23 AM. The apartment block is flattened to a rectangle of churned dirt and rocks; the streetlamp, buildings and cars are gone but trees, bike racks, a stop sign (street sign missing) and a booted moped remain. Car-shaped dirt indentations where vehicles were pulled through the asphalt. A distant fire burns. Wind smells like the ocean. A trumpet blast announces the entrances: bright pillars of light from holes in the ground (one inside a former thrift store). Carl wears boxers, a leather jacket and pink Crocs. A severed head (Mrs. Parsons) lies near his feet. Mood: silent, eerie, horrifying, then a rush toward warmth.
- **Entrance staircase.** [L235-L247] A wide (20-person) ornate wrought-iron stair, steps carved with fish/demon-like patterns, going down into a glowing warm light; air gets humid and about 80 F. At the bottom is a marble floor and a 30-foot wooden arched double door carved as a Kua-Tin fish demon. Tooltip jokes appear.
- **First hallway and tunnels.** [L267-L271] Behind the door: a torch-lit tunnel as wide and tall as a double-lane road, with branching corridors and a neon sign "Da Tutorial Guild" pointing down a dark alley (trap). After the door slams, the room goes dusk-dim. HUD timer appears (4d 23h 48m).
- **Goblin Murder Dozer scene.** [L369-L491, L553-L615] Goblins in a rusted steam-powered tractor. Side corridors: a narrow half-lit hall, a too-obvious dark hall, a lichen-lit brick hall with a plain red-painted "Tutorial Guild" door. The dozer explodes on the wall of the corridor outside the guild. Sets the tone for the Level 0 tutorial (a chase, a boss-like contraption, the safe room door).
- **Tutorial Guild room.** [L505-L645] Classroom-size stone room: fireplace, bed, shelves of photos of bird creatures and an urn, a gray oval carpet, a desk, scattered classroom chairs. Mordecai (Rat Hooligan). It is where inventory and menus are unlocked.

### Floor 1: the "city grid" floor (Seattle / Puget Sound area)

Look and mood: a labyrinth of underground corridors laid out in a giant grid of equal squares bounded by very wide "artery" tunnels (torch-lit, double-lane width). Each square is a twisty maze made of narrower brick tunnels lit by glowing green lichen; ceilings 15 feet or lower, walls dark-red brick or stone. Humid, stuffy, sometimes stinks of oil, sewage, rot. Four neighborhoods per square, each with its own boss lair. Random tutorial guilds (plain doors with the same Mordecai), bathrooms every quarter mile, and safe rooms made of real-world buildings dropped underground (a fast-food restaurant, a bus-station waiting room, a Taco Bell). Everything comes from Earth's buildings. Fog of war covers unexplored areas. Zones of notable look:

1. **Goblin neighborhood.** [L359-L615, L2487-L2815] Smells of machines, oil and pineapple. The goblin Workshop is a large room with a giant steam engine along one wall, six parked Murder Dozers, tables of tools, a mountain of coal, XXX barrels of "funpowder" and a sparks-showering boiler. Enemies: Goblins (Lv2), Goblin Engineers (Lv3, odd cookware helmets), Goblin Bomb Bards (Lv5, spiked German helmets, glass bombs), Shamankas (Lv7, pierced and robed). Boss: the Goblin War Chieftain (big goblin reading a book, babies and pregnant goblins present). Gimmick: goblin pass makes them friendly; Donut can charm; explosions are the preferred answer.
2. **Llama neighborhood.** [L1457-L1531] Across the arterial road from the goblins. Bad Llamas (Lv3) in alleys that shoot lava spit; they sell meth.
3. **The Hoarder neighborhood.** [L1599-L2135] Southeast corner of the first square. Rats, Scatterers, bathrooms. Boss lair: a 1,500 sq ft round room, 15-ft piles of garbage, old door with a Spanish health-department notice, skull-headed torches rising from trash, small fires. The Hoarder (Lv7).
4. **Safe room #1 (fast-food playground).** [L1611-L1795] A Polish-style (blue and white) fast-food restaurant, kids' play structure with slide and ball pit, windows showing only solid wall, three screens, 20 free rental sleeping cubicles, and a soda machine (Hoop Cola, water, milk, vodka, Warka beer). Tally cooks.

7. **Troglodyte neighborhood (gym).** [L3611-L3809] Troglodyte lizards; boss lair is a 24-hour gym.
8. **Taco Bell safe room.** [L3987] A Peruvian Taco Bell with beer and steak, Bopca Sebastian.
9. **Waffle House safe room (Alabama).** [L3489] Where about 24 elderly chose to stay and sing.
10. **Floor 1 Borough Boss spiral.** [L3299-L3355, L3981-L4209] A huge circle with an outer ring road, a drawbridge-like entrance, and multiple rounded portcullis windows (clay-like, glowing blue, impervious). After the gate closes the arena walls sink into the ground, revealing a roller-rink/rave dome: concentric grooves in the floor, rising and falling walls, black-light purple light, neon ceiling lights, lasers, thumping EDM bass. The stairwell glows at the center behind a red forcefield; the stairs have turned into a ramp. Boss: **The Ball of Swine** (Lv15).

## 8. Humor, tone, recurring bits

- The System's mood: sarcastic, vulgar, mocking; item descriptions are punchlines. Achievements insult the player ("You must really love that cat"). Mob tooltips use pop-culture jokes (incels, Circle K, chihuahuas, Cajun-speaking dingoes).
- Running gags: Carl never gets pants or shoes (he gets a toe ring, kneepads, then boxers); the System's foot fetish; "Goddamnit, Donut"; Donut's all-caps chat; Donut hates torches (she owns 230); Donut keeps mentioning an unexplained "Ferdinand"; Mongo bites Donut's nose; cockroaches and grubs explode; goblins always carry pineapples; Carl's cigarettes run out; Mordecai is allergic to cat and bitter about Odette.
- Recurring structure: "Time to Level Collapse" boxes, bold System announcements, "kill, kill, kill!" sign-offs, patch notes that join absurdity to tragedy (exploding toilets).
- Tone shifts: the book alternates between slapstick and genuine grief (the Hoarder, the goblin babies, Yolanda's death, Mrs. McGibbons). Carl's guilt and exhaustion keep the jokes from feeling empty.
- Quotes (short): "You will not break me" (Carl's mantra); "Goddamnit, Donut" (catchphrase); "Kill, kill, kill" (announcer sign-off); "Glurp, glurp" (Maestro's audience chant); "Today is the day you gain" (gym poster slogan); "It's not a pyramid" (Rev-Up pamphlet).

## 9. Ideas for the side-scroller

Level 0 notes: the existing street/stairs/tunnels maps well onto chapter 1-2. Possible add-ons: a flattened building lot with car-shaped dents and a stray bike rack; Mrs. Parsons's head as a one-time grim prop; the wrought-iron stair with fish carvings as a descending parallax set piece; the 30-ft Kua-Tin door as a boss-door gimmick (it slams shut); the dozer chase as the Level 0 finale (auto-scroller where the dozer follows).

1. **Goblin Murder Dozer boss.** A right-to-left chase (or an arena) where Carl must avoid a spiked-wheel steam tractor, bait it into walls, then jump on it and defeat the three riders before the "Boiler Breach" 12-second timer ends. Reward: pot helmet pickup.
2. **Tutorial Guild as a hub.** A small safe room with fireplace, Mordecai, and a locked door that will not open if enemies are in the hallway. Players restock and spend loot boxes here.
3. **Loot-box opening minigame.** Boxes pop in a row (bronze, silver, gold, legendary), open with music and a snarky announcer line; most items are torches and biscuits; a toe ring instead of shoes.
4. **Achievement popups in the System's voice.** Short, rude, tied to player behavior (taking damage, kicking a goblin, standing near an explosion). Achievement toast could show the box tier.
5. **Barefoot combat.** Carl's kicks and stomps scale with a Smush counter; stomping drops explosion gore effects; pedicure kit is an in-game buff.
6. **Donut's Magic Missile.** Two eye lasers; mana bar; potions have separate cooldown timers (long for Donut, short for Carl). Donut sits on Carl's head and fires; she jumps off to dodge and lands back.
7. **Palette: Floor 1.** Dark red brick, murky green lichen glow, torch orange, bone-white skulls; Floor 2: white floors, orange lichen, grey cinderblock, cyan fire; boss rooms flash neon.
8. **Boss intro cards.** Slam a mugshot VS screen with a metallic-blood font and a one-line description; door locks with glowing silver bars; boss music kicks in.
9. **Hoarder boss.** A garbage-pile arena with skull torches. The Hoarder vomits roaches; players must kill the bug in her mouth before it exits; non-lethal ending option is a gag (not recommended; keep tragic tone).
10. **Gym boss "The Juicer".** Mirrors, treadmills and racks; thrown flaming plates ricochet; the fog scroll blinds him; weak point is the neck vein.
11. **Ball of Swine.** A rolling hazard in a lane-switching spiral: the ball rolls across the foreground; the player drops spike strips and builds the Speedbump to wedge it; afterward tuxedoed orcs spill out as a mop-up wave.
12. **Safe-room set dressing.** Fast-food restaurants with playgrounds, Waffle House, Taco Bell; each has one weird perk (experience cookie plate, Mana Toast, mailbox that gives spells).
13. **Weird-mob catalog.** Bad Llama with lava spit, rot-sticker raviolis that chitter before blowing, Scatterer roaches, troglodytes, kobold chihuahua knights on dingoes, Clurichauns with slingshots, Laminak fairy managers, mind-brain blimps.
14. **Moonshine economy and Carl's Jug O' Boom.** Collect jugs, craft fire bombs; flame pool damages over time; corpses burn so loot is lost, adding a tradeoff.
15. **Krakaren Clone.** Tentacles burst through cinderblock walls, each mouth screams; player must craft an immunity smoothie at a bicycle blender before fighting; the "Taint" debuff disables healing.
16. **Rage Elemental chase.** Level-ending sequence: the player rides a steam bike (side-scrolling), drops bola chain, oil slick and bombs, jumps off before a stairwell hole. Great for a Level 4-5 set piece. Combined with Reverse Gravity for a vertical flipped section.
17. **Mongo the raptor.** A pet that fights on its own, bites Donut's nose for comic damage, and must be trained to "sic 'em". Has a bonded/unbonded state.
18. **TV interludes.** Cutscenes: Odette's holographic studio, the Maestro's Glurp audience (a darker, edgy variant), Zev popping in with a splash of water; viewers' counters (views/followers) tick up as scoring.
19. **Social score.** Add a views meter that rises when the player does flashy things (killing with style, using catchphrase) and falls if the player waits too long.
20. **Survivor counter.** A persistent HUD count of remaining humans falling during play ("clink, clink") as a creeping dread mechanic.

(Items 9-20 are suggestions drawn from the book, not canon.)
